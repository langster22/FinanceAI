AI-Enabled Future of Finance

What needs to be true

Five things have to be true to successfully achieve an AI-enabled future of Finance. Each builds on the one before it.

01

Set a clear strategy

Aligned to business outcomes with defined ownership and success measures.

  • Align with business goals — ensure your Finance AI strategy supports the business' objectives (revenue vs. cost).
  • Collaborate with technology partners — involve technology stakeholders early to drive buy-in.
  • Consider all enablers — assess AI's impact on Finance's capabilities, technologies, and talent.
02

Identify opportunities

Prioritize high-value use cases across the organization.

  • Assess top-down — identify process areas with the largest potential disruption from AI.
  • Reimagine capabilities — redesign future state end-to-end with AI as a critical enabler.
  • Start targeted; don't wait for vendor solutions to mature.
03

Define an investment strategy

Tie funding to measurable value, platforms, and governance.

  • Build foundations first: strategy & governance, data, technology.
  • Move to single-agent solutions before multi-agent orchestration.
  • Enterprise value compounds as the stack matures.
04

Monitor & realize value

Identify KPIs focused on tracking the value of AI investments.

  • Financial — revenue per token consumed, AI run-rate as % of revenue.
  • Operational — cycle-time reduction, time-to-value acceleration.
  • Strategic — customer value creation, AI-enabled capabilities, speed to market.
  • Employee — time on strategic work, engagement, time to proficiency.
05

Prepare Finance teams

Across technology, data, operating model, talent, and adoption.

  • Technology — assess system architecture for limits and accelerators.
  • Data — review the landscape and prioritize sources.
  • Operating model — adjust structure to be leaner and more adaptive.
  • Talent — understand if AI is a priority for Finance and what it means for the team.
  • Adoption — incorporate AI fluency into the CFO strategy.

AI-Enabled Future of Finance

How to invest

Defining a clear investment strategy means knowing where to start and what value to capture. The path is stepwise — foundations first, then agents, then orchestration — with value compounded at each stage.

Stage 01

Strategy & Governance

Set the direction, define ownership, agree on success measures.

  • Document Finance AI strategy and tie to business outcomes
  • Establish AI governance, risk, and oversight
  • Define investment thresholds and decision rights
Foundational · non-negotiable
Stage 02

Data Foundations

Get the data ready before the agents.

  • Inventory and prioritize Finance data sources
  • Address data quality, lineage, and access
  • Stand up a Finance data product layer
Without this, agents fail at scale
Stage 03

Technology Platforms

Modernize the stack to support AI workloads.

  • Cloud ERP and EPM modernization
  • Integrated analytics platform with single front door
  • LLM access and orchestration infrastructure
Enables single-agent solutions
Stage 04

Single-Agent Solutions

Deploy targeted AI agents against specific processes.

  • Policy assistants, controls monitoring, close co-pilots
  • Measure value per agent on the four KPI dimensions
  • Refine and scale what works; retire what doesn't
First measurable ROI
Stage 05

Multi-Agent Orchestration

Agents collaborate across processes to deliver end-to-end outcomes.

  • End-to-end orchestration across O2C, R2R, S2P
  • Agents that pass context, escalate exceptions, learn from each other
  • Finance function operating as a network of agents + humans
Compound enterprise value
Most Finance functions today sit at Stage 01–02. Few are past Stage 04 in production. Sequence matters as much as size.

Finance Transformation Approaches

Your path forward

Three pathways describe how Finance functions are approaching AI today. Most CFOs identify with one — and the synthesis below pulls together what you said about your readiness and where to start.

Pathway 01

The Technologist

Leads with platform modernization. Cloud ERP, EPM, data layer — get the foundations enterprise-grade, then build agents on top.

Strengths
  • Solid technical foundations
  • Clear architecture & governance
  • Scalable from day one
Watch out for
  • Long time-to-first-value
  • Business may lose patience
  • Tech-first risks ignoring talent
Pathway 02

The Collaborator

Leads with cross-functional partnership. Co-creates with the business, IT, and operations — value through orchestration, not just automation.

Strengths
  • Buy-in across the enterprise
  • Use cases tied to business outcomes
  • Talent & adoption baked in
Watch out for
  • Slower decision velocity
  • Risk of consensus paralysis
  • Hard to attribute value to Finance
Pathway 03

The Innovator

Leads with bold experiments. Targeted agents shipped fast, value measured early, scaled where it works — accept some misses.

Strengths
  • Fast value capture
  • Builds AI fluency through doing
  • Compelling proof points for the board
Watch out for
  • Technical debt accumulates
  • Hard to scale ad-hoc agents
  • Risk & controls can lag
